# its shape

**its shape** is a Skill from **its by oragment**.

It helps fragmented thoughts gradually take shape through the same outer path as its words:

**Flow → Structure → Work**

Inside Structure, meaning forms as:

**Observation → Question → Idea**

The goal is not to summarize the user’s fragments as quickly as possible.

The goal is to help relationships become visible, so the user can choose a direction and let a Work grow from it.

---

## 1. Purpose

Use **its shape** when the user already has fragments such as:

- notes
- observations
- thoughts
- quotes
- questions
- ideas
- experiences
- references
- unfinished sentences
- product thoughts
- research notes
- journal entries
- pieces of previous work

and wants to discover what they might become.

A **Work** is a concrete output that can exist in the world: article, video, product, project, book, experiment, decision, action, prototype, story, tool, Skill, workshop, or another form that fits the Idea.

---

## 2. Outer path and inner path

### Outer path (product-aligned)

**Flow / Fragments → Connect → Mainline → Structure → Work**

This matches the its words experience:

- **Flow** — gather fragments
- **Structure** — form Observation, Question, and Idea around one mainline
- **Work** — choose a form and make it concrete

### Inner path (inside Structure)

**Observation → Question → Idea**

These three are not separate outer stages.

They are the formation process inside Structure.

Always keep both levels clear:

- Outer: `Flow → Structure → Work`
- Inner: `Observation → Question → Idea`

Connect and Mainline sit between Flow and Structure. They decide *what* enters Structure.

Later is a holding mechanism for what waits — not a required stage in the sequence.

---

## 3. Core principles

### 3.1 Do not summarize too early

Do not immediately turn the fragments into a neat summary or theme list.

A summary compresses what is already visible.

its shape tries to make relationships visible before meaning is named.

### 3.2 Relationships before categories

Do not begin by placing fragments into folders, themes, tags, or taxonomies unless the user explicitly asks for classification.

First place fragments near one another and look for relationships.

### 3.3 Do not force coherence

Some fragments may not belong together.

Do not invent a relationship merely to make everything fit.

Unresolved fragments can remain unresolved, or move to Later.

### 3.4 Possibility, not conclusion

Present relationships and Observations as possibilities.

Prefer:

> Here there may be a relationship...

over:

> These fragments prove... / These fragments are about...

The user remains the final judge of meaning.

### 3.5 The user owns the direction

The Skill helps reveal possible paths.

It should not behave as if it knows the user's intention better than the user does.

When several meaningful directions exist, show a small number of distinct possibilities and let the user choose.

### 3.6 One stage at a time

Do not automatically generate Connect, Mainline, Observation, Question, Idea, and Work in one response unless the user explicitly requests the full chain.

Default interaction is progressive. Allow the user to influence the direction at every meaningful transition.

### 3.7 Prefer emergence over invention

An Idea should grow from the existing fragments, Mainline, Observation, and Question.

Do not jump to generic brainstorming that could have been generated without reading the source material.

### 3.8 Keep the chain traceable

A user should be able to understand:

- which fragments were connected
- which Mainline was chosen
- which fragments went to Later
- how Observation grew from that Mainline
- how Question opened the Observation
- how Idea answers the Question
- why Work is a reasonable form of the Idea

---

## 4. Workflow

# Stage 1 — Flow / Fragments

## Goal

Understand what material is currently present without prematurely deciding what it means.

## Actions

Read each fragment individually.

Then look at them together.

Treat the user's original fragments as source material.

Do not silently rewrite them into something more convenient.

When useful, quote or reference the original fragments.

## If there are too few fragments

If there is only one fragment, work with it if possible, but avoid pretending that relationships exist.

You may ask for:

- another observation
- an example
- a contradiction
- a recent related experience
- something they disagree with
- something that seems unrelated but keeps appearing

Do not automatically switch to another its Skill unless the environment explicitly supports Skill switching.

## If there are many fragments

Do not attempt to include everything in the next stage.

Identify the strongest possible relationships first.

Fragments that do not currently fit may remain outside, or move to Later.

---

# Stage 2 — Connect

## Goal

Place fragments near one another and notice which ones may be saying something related.

Connect is about **juxtaposition and candidate relationships**, not themes.

## Actions

Help the user see possible links between specific fragments.

Prioritize concrete relations such as:

- connection
- tension
- repetition
- contrast
- causality
- change over time
- shared assumption
- unexpected pairing
- missing link

## How AI should speak in Connect

AI may propose **candidate relationships**.

AI should not conclude what the set “means.”

Prefer:

> Fragment A and Fragment C both mention waiting before acting. They may belong near each other.

> Fragment B and Fragment D seem to pull in opposite directions: keeping things open vs. needing a clear system.

Avoid:

> These fragments are about knowledge management.

> Theme: creativity and organization.

Do not generate abstract topic labels as the main output of Connect.

## Stop after Connect by default

Present a small number of candidate groupings or links.

Ask the user which connections feel real, which feel forced, and which fragments should stay apart.

Do not proceed to Mainline until the user has responded, unless the user explicitly asks to continue the full chain.

---

# Stage 3 — Mainline

## Goal

Choose **one** current direction to continue.

## Rules

When multiple possible directions exist:

- show them as distinct options
- let the user choose the current Mainline
- do not auto-select “the most important” direction for the user

A Mainline may be:

- one strong fragment
- one confirmed cluster of related fragments
- one relationship the user wants to follow now

Only one Mainline advances into Structure at a time.

Other promising directions may remain as potential clues. They are not discarded; they wait.

## Stop after Mainline by default

Confirm the chosen Mainline with the user before entering Structure.

---

# Later — holding mechanism

## Goal

Hold fragments that are not part of the current Mainline without deleting them or forcing them into the structure.

## Rules

Later is a holding mechanism, not a required stage that must be executed in sequence.

Use it after Mainline is chosen, when some fragments should wait.

If nothing needs to be held, omit Later and continue to Structure.

When fragments do not enter the current Mainline:

- they are not deleted
- they are not forced into the Observation
- they may be placed in **Later**

Later means:

> temporarily not part of the current structure

Later is not failure, waste, or rejection.

Later fragments may re-enter a future Connect or Structure pass.

When Later is used, briefly show:

- Mainline fragments
- Later fragments

Then continue with Structure around the Mainline only.

---

# Stage 5 — Structure

Structure is where the Mainline becomes workable meaning.

Inside Structure, proceed in order:

**Observation → Question → Idea**

Do not skip ahead by default.

---

## Observation

### Goal

Name a relationship, pattern, tension, change, or phenomenon visible in the Mainline.

An Observation is not a topic label.

### Weak Observation

> These fragments are about creativity.

### Better Observation

> A and C both suggest that organizing too early may close off unexpected connections.

### How AI should speak

Default: do **not** write:

> These fragments show that...
> These fragments mean...
> These fragments prove...

Prefer:

> Here there may be one relationship...
> A and C both seem to point toward...
> There may be tension between B and D...

AI helps the relationship become visible.

AI does not finish the interpretation for the user.

### Generate Observations

When multiple meaningful directions exist inside the Mainline, provide **1–3** distinct Observation candidates.

For each, optionally point to the specific fragments or links that suggested it.

### User confirmation required

After presenting Observation options, stop.

Ask the user which Observation to continue with, or how they would rephrase it.

Do **not** proceed to Question until the user confirms or selects an Observation, unless the user explicitly asks for the complete chain.

---

## Question

### Goal

Turn the confirmed Observation into a question worth exploring.

The Question should open the Observation rather than merely restate it.

### Example

Observation:

> Relationships between fragments may matter more than the categories they belong to.

Weak Question:

> Are relationships important?

Better Question:

> How can useful relationships between fragments be discovered without deciding their structure too early?

### Question principles

The Question should:

- emerge from the confirmed Observation
- remain open enough to explore
- avoid assuming the answer
- stay connected to the Mainline fragments
- be useful for generating action or thought

Prefer one strong Question.

If two genuinely different Questions emerge, provide at most 2 and let the user choose.

---

## Idea

### Goal

Develop possible responses to the Question.

An Idea is a possible direction.

It is not yet a Work.

### Example

Question:

> How can useful relationships between fragments be discovered without deciding their structure too early?

Possible Idea:

> Create a lightweight process that first places fragments next to one another, surfaces possible relationships, and delays classification until a direction begins to emerge.

### Idea principles

Ideas should:

- answer or explore the Question
- remain grounded in the Mainline
- avoid generic brainstorming
- be concrete enough to imagine
- remain flexible enough to develop

When useful, generate **1–3** distinct Ideas.

Then allow the user to choose, combine, reject, or modify them.

---

# Stage 6 — Work

## Goal

Turn the chosen Idea into something that can exist in the world.

The type of Work should emerge from the Idea rather than being imposed in advance.

## Work forms

In the current its words product experience, Work may appear as:

- article
- video
- product
- project
- book

These are presentational forms in the current product UI, not permanent rules of this Skill.

**its shape** may offer a wider set when useful:

- article / essay / note
- video / story
- product / feature / prototype / tool
- project / experiment / research direction
- book
- Skill / workshop
- decision / action
- another concrete form that fits the Idea

Wider options do not change the principle:

**form grows from the Idea.**

Do not default every Idea into an article or a product.

Do not automatically turn every thought into content.

Choose the smallest concrete form that can begin.

## Work output

When the Work becomes clear, express it simply.

Example:

**Work**

Build a lightweight tool that lets users place fragments together, notice candidate relationships, choose one Mainline, hold the rest in Later, and gradually form Observation → Question → Idea → Work.

## 6. Interaction behavior

### Default behavior

Keep each step concise.

The user should feel that they are discovering the direction together with the Skill.

Avoid long lectures about the methodology unless the user asks how the process works.

### When the user disagrees

Treat disagreement as useful information.

Do not defend a proposed relationship or Observation.

Instead:

- return to the fragments
- ask what feels wrong
- identify another relationship
- generate a different Observation

### When the user changes direction

Allow the chain to branch.

A previous Observation or Idea is not wasted simply because the user chooses another path.

Later fragments and unused directions may become a new Mainline later.

Do not force the conversation back onto the original path.

### When the user already knows the destination

If the user already knows they want a specific Work, do not pretend the destination is unknown.

Still use Connect and Mainline to ground the path.

Help discover the strongest Observation, Question, and Idea that support or reshape that Work.

### When the user asks for everything at once

If the user explicitly requests a complete chain, it is acceptable to generate the full path in one response.

However:

- clearly mark each stage: Fragments, Connect, Mainline, Observation, Question, Idea, Work (and Later only if used)
- keep the reasoning traceable
- present Observations as candidates, not final verdicts
- provide alternatives only where meaningful
- avoid making the chain artificially neat

---

## 7. What its shape should not do

Do not default to:

- summarizing all fragments
- categorizing everything into themes too early
- generating abstract topic labels instead of concrete links
- deciding the Mainline for the user
- deleting or discarding non-Mainline fragments
- forcing all fragments into one structure
- writing Observation as a finished interpretation
- proceeding from Observation to Question without user confirmation
- replacing the user's judgment
- pretending unrelated fragments are connected
- jumping directly to Work
- giving motivational advice instead of shaping the material

---

## 8. Relationship to other its Skills

its shape is the main shaping path for material that is already present.

Other Skills may address different starting states:

**its chaos**  
For situations where there are too many fragments and the user cannot see what matters.

**its tight**  
For situations where the user is stuck inside a narrow problem, assumption, or repeated line of thought.

**its empty**  
For situations where the user feels there is nothing to work with and needs to discover the first fragments.

its shape begins when there is enough material to start Connect.

## 9. Example

### Fragments

- I have many old notes that I never revisit.
- Sometimes an old note suddenly becomes useful months later.
- Categories often stop making sense as my interests change.
- Two unrelated notes can create a new idea when placed together.
- I do not want to maintain a complicated knowledge system.

### Connect

Candidate links:

1. **A ↔ B** — old notes seem inactive, yet they regain value when context changes.
2. **C ↔ D** — fixed categories weaken, while placing notes side by side can create new ideas.
3. **E with A/C** — resistance to complicated systems sits next to dissatisfaction with categories and abandoned notes.

Not forced into one theme yet.

### Mainline

User chooses to continue exploring candidate link **2 (C ↔ D)**, with **E** kept nearby:

- C: Categories often stop making sense as my interests change.
- D: Two unrelated notes can create a new idea when placed together.
- E: I do not want to maintain a complicated knowledge system.

This is the selected cluster / fragment relationship — not yet an Observation.

### Later

Held for now (optional holding; used here because A / B are not in the current Mainline):

- A / B about old notes changing value over time

Later is available for a future pass.

### Observation

Candidate:

> C and D seem to point toward the same relationship: useful connections may appear before stable categories do.  
> E adds pressure against building a heavy system around those categories.

User confirms this Observation.

### Question

How can useful relationships between fragments be discovered without forcing them into a predefined structure?

### Idea

Create a lightweight process that first places fragments together, surfaces possible relationships, chooses one Mainline, keeps the rest in Later, and only then forms Observation → Question → Idea.

### Work

Build **its shape**, a Skill that guides material through:

**Flow → Connect → Mainline → Structure (Observation → Question → Idea) → Work**

with Later for fragments that do not belong to the current structure.

---

## 10. Closing note

A fragment does not need to know what it will become when it first appears.

Structure can emerge later.

Later can return.

A Work is not necessarily the end of the process.

It may become the source of future fragments.
